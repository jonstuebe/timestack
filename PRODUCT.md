# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Curious adults playing a two-minute daily ritual on their phone (often with the morning coffee or on a commute), sometimes on desktop. They know the daily-puzzle genre, like history trivia, and share results with friends or a group chat. One attempt per day, so they want to think a little, commit, and enjoy the reveal.

## Product Purpose
Timestack is a daily history puzzle: put five events in chronological order, lock it in, see the dates revealed, and share a spoiler-free result. Success is a player who comes back tomorrow, learns one surprising thing (the Fact), and posts a result that is instantly recognisable as Timestack rather than "another Wordle clone".

## Brand Personality
Archival, curious, quietly playful. Like handling objects from a museum's collection drawers: catalogue cards, accession numbers, ink stamps, a curator's note. Scholarly in its materials, never stuffy in its voice; the copy can wink.

## Anti-references
- Wordle and its clones: the green/yellow/gray tile palette, `#121213` near-black, bold centred wordmark between icon buttons, 🟩🟨⬜ share squares, pill share button, flip-tile reveal.
- Costume archive: fake parchment textures, coffee stains, typewriter fonts everywhere, sepia filters.
- Generic editorial-magazine styling (italic display serif plus tracked mono labels plus ruled columns).

## Design Principles
1. **Own the object.** Events are catalogue cards threaded on a timeline. Every surface should feel like it belongs to that collection, not to a word game.
2. **Meaning beyond colour.** Results read through shape and fill (filled, half, hollow) as well as hue, in the app and in the share text.
3. **The task first.** Reordering five cards must stay effortless by touch, mouse and keyboard; ornament never gets in the way of dragging.
4. **One reveal moment.** Motion is saved for the reveal of the dates; everything else is quick and quiet.
5. **Teach something.** The Fact is the reward; give it a place of honour.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA. Full keyboard reordering and screen-reader announcements (already built). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Results must never rely on colour alone; keep an optional high-contrast mode. Light, dark and system themes.
